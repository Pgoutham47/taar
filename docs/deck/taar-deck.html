Two populations, and a gap between them.

A factor of three between the populations, no overlap. The threshold moved to 6× because of this plot — the previous value, chosen from simulation, sat close enough to the idle readings that a dead circuit could read as live.

One capture in the lower lane read 1×. It was taken in the same minute as the last idle reading, before the compressor had started, and a fridge draws almost nothing until it does. We set it aside on an explanation formed after the fact, and the separation depends on that being right.

The proof Unretouched screen captures

Two pairs, taken minutes apart. The phone was taped down and not touched; the only thing that changed in the room was a switch at the wall.

Both numbers come off the device, not from a model. Contrast 2–3× with no current; 9–16× with the compressor drawing. Field amplitude 0.05 µT against 0.10–0.14, on a noise floor we measured at 0.40.

What this does not show is a sensitivity figure. One appliance, roughly 1–1.5 A, through a cable whose live and neutral largely cancel — enough to establish the effect, not to quantify it.

03
Why it works

Two physical facts nobody has pointed a phone at.

B = μ₀I / 2πr

A conductor broadcasts its current

At three centimetres, five amperes produce about 33 µT. Earth's field is 45 µT and the sensor floor is 0.40. The signal is not small — it is the same order as the planet.

This is the principle a clamp meter uses. Taar does it without the clamp, which is the whole product.

100 Hz = 2 × mains

An arc broadcasts its fault

A series arc re-ignites twice per cycle, so its broadband noise is amplitude-modulated at 100 Hz. We look for energy at that frequency, not for energy.

Against four confounders — a ballast hum, speech, a sleeve brushing the phone, a motor — a plain energy detector fired on every one, every time. Ours held its 5% design point.

Both run on the device, and that is not a scoring choice. Distribution boards live in basements with no signal. A building's circuit layout is not a contractor's data to upload. And a measurement of response time cannot have a network round trip inside it.

04
Who it is for

The instruments cost more than the firm earns in a month.

A clamp meter, a thermal camera and an arc-fault analyser are what make a loose connection visible before it starts a fire. For a two-person electrical firm that is a capital outlay they cannot justify, so the tools stay with large contractors and the buildings that most need checking never get checked.

Every one of those electricians already carries a magnetometer and a microphone. Taar is the argument that the instrument is already in their pocket.

05
What hardware taught us Four defects · each now covered by a test

Everything we got wrong, we got wrong in simulation.

The app passed every check we had written and still returned nothing on a real phone. These four only appeared once it was installed.

01
Two Android clocks
SensorEvent.timestamp counts deep sleep; System.nanoTime() does not. Comparing them ended every capture on its first sample.
02
Magnitude destroyed the signal
Reducing three axes to √(x²+y²+z²) hid the AC component behind Earth's static field. A 1 µT signal moved it by 0.01.
03
Normalised by the wrong quantity
A hand-held capture drifts several µT. That drift buried the 50 Hz term and reported a real signal as zero confidence.
04
A threshold we guessed
The arc floor was five times too small, and called a domestic fridge an arcing fault. Re-set from nine measured captures.

A simulation that had predicted any of these would have been a better simulation. It did not — which is the case for building early and putting it on the hardware, and the reason we would rather show this list than claim the first build worked.

Signal path

Per-axis sine fit at 50 Hz, detrended, amplitude as the vector sum. Lomb–Scargle contrast against 30–48 and 52–70 Hz. Scoring by median and MAD against the circuit's own reference.

On top of it

A rules table that shows its evidence. A nearest-centroid classifier trained on the device from the technician's labels, which rejects rather than guessing. No language model is built; one is planned for the build window.

07
Scope

What Taar does not do.

It is a triage aid, not a certification

It does not replace a licensed electrician, a calibrated clamp meter, an insulation tester or a statutory inspection. The app says so on the result screen, not in a settings page.

The acoustic half is designed, not validated

No real arc has been recorded. The magnetometer half rests on textbook physics and now on measurement; the arc half rests on neither yet, and we will say so on stage before a judge asks.

It reports an index, not amperes

Unless the circuit has been calibrated against a known load. A fabricated ampere figure is worse than no figure.

It refuses rather than guesses

With no reference, or an ill-conditioned fit, it returns a refusal. An electrician acting on a confident wrong reading is the worst outcome this project has, and the code is arranged so that outcome is unreachable.

Our pre-event work is a research spike on a separate branch, rebuilt from an empty scaffold inside the event window and never merged. The protocol is written down, and we will confirm it with an organiser at check-in.
