01 · The problem

Electrical faults start fires. The faults that start them are invisible.

A loose connection, quietly arcing

Behind a switchboard cover, a terminal that was tightened ten years ago sparks a hundred times a second. Nobody hears it. It heats, chars and one day ignites.

A circuit run over its rating

An AC and a geyser were added to a wire sized for lights. The breaker holds, the insulation slowly cooks. The bill goes up and nobody knows why.

A breaker labelled “off” that is live

Before touching a wire, an electrician needs to know it is dead. The usual method is a label on a board that may be a decade out of date, or a probe.

The instruments that would catch this — a clamp meter, a thermal camera, an arc-fault analyser — cost more than a small electrician earns in a month.

So the tools stay with large contractors, and the homes and small shops that most need checking never get checked. A family has no way at all to ask “is something wrong inside my wall?”

03 · The insight

Every live wire broadcasts its current as a magnetic field.
Every sparking joint broadcasts its fault as sound.

A phone already carries both instruments.

The compass sensor is a current sensor

The magnetometer that points Google Maps north also feels the 50 Hz wobble of a mains wire. The principle a ₹5,000 clamp meter uses, without the clamp.

The microphone is an arc detector

A series arc re-ignites twice per mains cycle. Its hiss pulses at exactly 100 Hz. Nothing else in a room is locked to the grid like that.

04 · Physics I · Current · measure the 50 Hz in the field, not the field

Only one thing in the room wobbles at exactly 50 Hz.

A wire carrying current makes a magnetic field, B = μ₀I / 2πr. A 5 A kettle at 3 cm gives about 33 µT, comparable to Earth's own 45 µT and far above the 0.4 µT sensor floor we measured. Strength was never the problem.

Telling it apart is. Earth's field is static, a hand drifts several µT and trembles at about 10 Hz, chargers and metal all move the needle. So Taar fits a 50 Hz sine to each axis and compares its power with the neighbouring bands, 30–48 and 52–70 Hz. That ratio is the contrast: room noise reads 1–3×, a kettle cord 28–62×.

05 · Physics II · The sampling trap that would have killed the project

Android's default sensor rate is 100 Hz. It cannot see 50 Hz.

Everyone assumes the danger is a slow sensor. It is not. What matters is how many distinct phases of the 50 Hz wave the samples land on:

phases = fs / gcd(fs, 50)      // need ≥ 3 to fit sin + cos

At 50 Hz every sample lands on the same phase and the wave reads as a constant. 24 Hz recovers it to 0.3%. Locked rates below 150 Hz: 10, 20, 25, 50, 100. The fix is one call at sensor registration: safeRate() moves 100 → 99. The iQOO delivered 105.3 Hz over 21 phases.

06 · Physics III · Sparking · an arc has a heartbeat

A sparking joint re-ignites twice every mains cycle. Its hiss pulses at 100 Hz.

The microphone records 3 s. Taar keeps only the 4–16 kHz band where arc noise lives, takes the envelope, and asks how much of that envelope pulses at 100 Hz compared with 20–400 Hz. Loudness cancels out, so one threshold serves a quiet cupboard and a plant room.

08 · The solution · Four taps to a result

Set up once. Then three seconds per wire.

STEP 1 · ONCE

Phone check

3 s flat on a table. Measures the real sensor rate, picks a safe one, reads the noise floor, checks the mic.

STEP 2 · ONCE PER BOARD

Name the circuits

One board, its breakers. Type the rating printed on each, e.g. B16, so Taar can warn near the limit.

STEP 3 · ONCE PER WIRE

Record its normal

3 quick captures with the wire in its everyday state. Every later reading is compared with this.

EVERY TIME

Measure

Say if the supply is on or off. Press the phone flat on the cable, hold still for 3 s. One combined result, spoken aloud.

Why a phone check first

Because 100 Hz, the Android default, cannot see 50 Hz. Taar measures what the sensor really delivers, moves it to a safe rate if needed, and shows the number to the user. It refuses to measure on a sensor it has not checked.

Explainable sensor fusion. It says what was observed, never “safe”.

The six warnings, each ending in an action

!Current on a circuit you switched off. Stop. Mislabelled breaker or back-feed. Do not work on it.
!Possible arcing / loose connection. Isolate and have the terminations checked.
!Load at or above breaker rating. Move load or have the circuit uprated.
!Load higher than usual. If you just switched something on, expected. If not, check what was added.
!Can't confirm the switched-off circuit. Keep still and measure again. Do not assume.
!Reading not usable. Re-run the phone check and capture again.

Three rules that never bend

✓Refuse rather than guess. No reference, an unclear signal or a moved phone gives a refusal, not a plausible number.
✓A warning only fires when every condition holds. A partial match is a different situation, not a weak diagnosis.
✓Amperes only when calibrated. Otherwise a relative index. A fabricated ampere figure is worse than none.

Tap “Why” on any result and every signal is listed with its verdict: supports, against, neutral or unavailable. A technician can disagree with the tool on site, because the tool showed its working.

What hardware taught us that simulation could not · four defects, each now a regression test

1

Two Android clocks

Sensor timestamps include deep sleep; the system clock does not. Every capture returned zero samples.

2

Magnitude erased the signal

Reducing x, y, z to one length hid a 1 µT wobble behind Earth's 45 µT. Each axis is now fitted separately.

3

Normalised by total variance

Hand drift buried the 50 Hz term and reported a real signal as zero. Now measured against neighbouring frequencies.

4

Guessed thresholds

The arc floor was five times too small and called a fridge an arcing fault. Every threshold is now set from captures.

15 · Scope, said out loud

What Taar does not do.

✕It does not certify. A triage aid. It does not replace a licensed electrician, a calibrated clamp meter, an insulation tester or a statutory inspection. The result screen says so.
✕It senses current, not voltage. A switched-off appliance on a live wire shows no current. Always use a voltage tester before touching anything.
✕It does not report amperes unless the circuit has been calibrated against a known load. Otherwise a relative index.
✕The arc model has never heard a real arc. It was trained on simulated arcs played through a speaker and recorded on a real phone, with real not-arcs. The pipeline retrains on a dimmer-rig recording unchanged.
✕It does not know where a cable runs inside a wall. House View is a diagram the way a metro map is a diagram.
✕The assistant can be wrong. It explains from the evidence, never decides, and is flagged when it sounds certain about safety.
Judges see a day of projects that claim more than they do. A team that draws its own boundary is the one they remember.
